Portfolio Management

Market Timing with Macroeconomic Signals

This project evaluated whether macroeconomic indicators could predict S&P 500 excess returns to enable strategic market timing. A regression model using lagged variables, including term premium, dividend yield, and industrial growth guided dynamic monthly allocation between S&P 500 and T-bills. In-sample strategy achieved a Sharpe ratio of 0.315, slightly outperforming the passive S&P 500 (Sharpe: 0.275), with final wealth of $5.03 vs. $4.56 for every $1 invested.

However, out-of-sample performance from 2007–2011 declined (Sharpe: 0.167, Wealth: $1.26), indicating weaker predictability. The model’s real time applicability is constrained by economic uncertainty and signal instability.

Pricing Strategy

Route-Level Pricing Optimization Strategy

This project assessed Ryanair’s pricing strategy through route-level regression on 537,000+ data points from the OAG Airfare Database. Focusing on the Rome–London Stansted route, results showed a positive correlation between fare and route length (β = 0.86) and a negative correlation between dynamic pricing and both flight frequency (β = -0.42) and route competition (β = -0.63).

As flight load increased, fares rose closer to departure, confirming yield management principles. Findings highlight how Ryanair balances cost leadership with demand-sensitive price discrimination to optimize revenue and maintain competitive edge across over 1,400 European routes.
